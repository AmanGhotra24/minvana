Sell Shovels, Not Winners: AI's NASDAQ Stocks

Stop trying to pick the AI winner. The money is in the shovels — chips, memory, power, cooling — and every one of them trades on NASDAQ.

60 SEC Your 60 Seconds

  • The thesis: every AI on Earth needs four physical things — chips to think, memory to remember, electricity to run, cooling to survive. Whichever company wins AI, the suppliers get paid first.
  • Power is the new bottleneck: data centers use about 4% of US electricity today; EPRI estimates 9–17% by 2030. Big Tech hyperscalers are spending roughly $750 billion in 2026 alone building AI data centers.
  • Chips: NVIDIA (NVDA, ~75% of AI accelerators), AMD (AMD). Memory: Micron (MU) — one of three makers of AI's HBM memory, 2026 supply sold out. Networking: Broadcom (AVGO), Marvell (MRVL). Servers: Supermicro (SMCI).
  • Power: Constellation Energy (CEG) — 20-year Microsoft deal, restarting the Three Mile Island nuclear plant. Chip-making machines: Applied Materials (AMAT), Lam Research (LRCX).
  • All nine trade on NASDAQ. One honest gap: the purest data-center cooling play, Vertiv, trades on the NYSE instead.

0:00 Don't pick the winner

Everyone is trying to pick the winner of the AI race. But the oldest rule of every gold rush still applies: the surest money isn't in the prospectors — it's in the companies selling the shovels. In the AI boom, the shovels are physical: chips to think, memory to remember, electricity to run, and cooling so it doesn't melt.

0:20 Power is the bottleneck

Wall Street's latest obsession isn't chips anymore — it's electricity. Analysts now call power the biggest bottleneck in AI. Data centers already consume about 4% of America's electricity, and the Electric Power Research Institute estimates that could reach 9–17% by 2030. The five big hyperscalers are spending roughly $750 billion in 2026 alone just building AI data centers.

0:40 The shovels, layer by layer

Chips: NVIDIA owns roughly three-quarters of the AI accelerator market; AMD chases. Memory: Micron is one of only three companies on Earth making high-bandwidth HBM memory — and its 2026 supply is effectively sold out. Networking: Broadcom and Marvell move data between thousands of GPUs. Servers: Supermicro houses it all. Power: Constellation Energy signed a 20-year deal with Microsoft and is restarting the Three Mile Island nuclear plant. And the machines that build the chips: Applied Materials and Lam Research. Every one of these trades on NASDAQ.

0:60 The gold-rush rule

You don't have to predict which AI lab wins. Whichever model dominates, the shovel-sellers get paid first. That's why Zacks, the Motley Fool, and TheStreet have all run recent features on AI infrastructure beyond the GPU trade.
