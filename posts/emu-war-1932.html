The Great Emu War of 1932: When Australia Lost to Birds

Twenty thousand emus trampled Western Australia's wheat fields. The army answered with machine guns. After a month of "war," the birds were still standing — and the soldiers went home.

0:00 The invasion

After World War One, the Australian government settled returning soldiers on farmland in Western Australia's wheatbelt. The land was dry and hard — and every year, emus migrated through it. In 1932, the migration was enormous: roughly 20,000 emus descended on the Campion district, trampling wheat flat and punching holes in fences that let rabbits in to finish the job. The ex-soldier farmers, struggling through the Great Depression, sent a deputation to the government. Their request: machine guns.

0:20 The deployment

Remarkably, the government agreed. Defence Minister Sir George Pearce approved the operation, and in November 1932 Major Gwynydd Purves Wynne-Aubrey Meredith of the Royal Australian Artillery arrived with two Lewis guns and 10,000 rounds. On November 2, the soldiers ambushed a flock of about 50 emus near Campion. The birds scattered instantly, and only a handful were hit. The guns — designed for trench warfare, not wildlife — jammed in the outback dust.

0:40 The birds adapt faster than the army

What followed was a month of humiliation. The emus split into small, fast, alert groups that scattered the moment firing started. Meredith mounted a Lewis gun on a truck to chase them — but the rough ground made aiming impossible, and the birds simply outran it. Between November 2 and 8, the men fired 2,500 rounds for only about 200 kills. Parliament paused the operation on November 8, then — under pressure from farmers — sent Meredith back on November 12. By the end, his final report claimed 9,860 rounds expended and 986 emus killed: exactly ten rounds per bird. Against 20,000 emus, it was nothing. Meredith reportedly admitted the birds faced machine guns with the maneuverability of tanks — and confirmed his own unit had suffered no casualties.

1:00 The surrender

On December 10, 1932, Meredith was recalled and the force withdrew. The media had a field day — the "Emu War" became a national joke and an enduring meme. Further requests for military help were refused. Instead, the government introduced a bounty system and exclusion fences; in 1934 alone, bounty hunters claimed over 57,000 emus. The military never fought birds again. Final score: Emus 1, Australia 0.
