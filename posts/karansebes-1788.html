The Battle of Karánsebes (1788): The Night an Army Killed Itself

One hundred thousand Austrian soldiers marched to fight the Ottoman Empire. Before the enemy even arrived, they had fought each other — 10,000 casualties, zero enemies. This is the comedy of errors history calls a battle.

0:00 The army that killed itself

In 1788, Austria was at war with the Ottoman Empire. Emperor Joseph II personally led a colossal force — some 100,000 men — east toward the town of Karánsebes. The army was a logistical marvel and a linguistic nightmare: Austrians, Germans, Czechs, Serbs, Croats, Poles, Italians, Romanians and Hungarians marched together, many of them unable to understand each other's orders. That detail is the whole story.

0:20 It started with schnapps

A detachment of cavalry scouts crossed the Timiș River and found locals selling schnapps. They bought the lot and got gloriously drunk. When infantry columns arrived and wanted their share, the cavalry refused — some accounts say they built fortifications around the barrels. The argument became a fistfight. Then, in the dark, a shot was fired. Panic did the rest: someone screamed "Turks! Turks!" and the camp exploded.

0:40 "Halt!" becomes "Allah!"

Here is where a bar brawl became a battle. Officers screamed "Halt!" to restore order — but across an army speaking a dozen languages, thousands of soldiers heard "Allah!" They were certain the Ottoman attack had begun. Regiments opened fire on shadows and on each other. Artillery crews, equally blind, shelled their own infantry. Emperor Joseph II, riding through the chaos, was nearly trampled by his own fleeing men and tumbled off his horse into a stream. The friendly fire raged all night.

1:00 The easiest victory in Ottoman history

By morning, the field was littered with Austrian dead and wounded — historians commonly cite as many as 10,000 casualties, making it one of the worst friendly-fire disasters ever recorded. The surviving army scattered. Two days later, the Ottoman forces arrived at Karánsebes, found it abandoned, and simply walked in — capturing the town without firing a single shot. Austria had defeated Austria, and handed its enemy a victory for free.
