Trump Says Russia Will Sell America Diesel

October 9–10, 2026 — Washington and Moscow

Your 60 seconds

After a call with Vladimir Putin, Donald Trump posted that Russia would immediately supply over 300,000 tons of diesel to American and global markets. That same afternoon, the Treasury issued General License 135, letting Russian diesel into the US through April 7, 2027. The next day, Russia partly lifted its own diesel export ban — in place since July 2026 after Ukrainian strikes on its refineries — authorizing 500,000 tons for foreign markets. That first 300,000-ton shipment is about 14 hours of what America burns. Russia gets an export door open; Trump gets a cheaper-fuel promise before the November midterms. Ukraine's president called the deal "unworthy of our partners."

0:00 — The announcement

On Friday, October 9, following a phone call with Putin, Trump wrote on Truth Social that Russia would "immediately supply over 300,000 Tons of Diesel Fuel to the American and Global Marketplace," with 500,000 tons in November and a million tons "immediately thereafter" — and up to 3 million more depending on the state of Russia's refineries. The quantities are announced commitments, not confirmed deliveries.

0:15 — The license, then the ban-lift

The same afternoon, the Treasury's OFAC issued General License 135, authorizing transactions in Russian-origin diesel — sale, delivery, offloading, import — through April 7, 2027. The next day, Russia's government partially lifted its diesel export ban, clearing 500,000 tons for foreign buyers effective October 10. That's the order it happened in: the American license first, then Russia's ban came off.

0:30 — How much is 300,000 tons?

About 2.25 million barrels — roughly 14 hours of US diesel consumption (the US burns around 4 million barrels a day, per EIA comparisons). It does not replace the roughly 800,000 barrels a day of Russian diesel exports that vanished when Moscow banned exports in July 2026. Deputy PM Alexander Novak said Russia's domestic market would stay "fully supplied" and supplies to the US could start in October.

0:40 — Who gets what

Russia gets an open door — and sanctions relief through April 2027 — for diesel it has been barred from exporting since July. Trump gets a promise of cheaper fuel weeks before the November midterms, with US diesel prices surging. Zelensky called it "unworthy of our partners" and "a weak decision," warning it hands Moscow resources for the war.

What we're watching

How much of the diesel actually reaches America — the license covers the whole world market — and whether Russia's refineries, still under Ukrainian drone attack, can deliver the bigger tranches. No public Russian concession has been confirmed.
