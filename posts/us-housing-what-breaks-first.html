What Breaks First

October 2026 — United States

Your 60 seconds

So what breaks first — sellers, or rates? Take the rate path: the Fed hiked again in September, 16 of 18 officials want one more hike this year, and the Fed's own forecast sees rates easing only gradually through 2028. The forecasters put mortgages in the mid sixes next year — at best. The rate path is closed. So what about prices? The math says a home would have to fall 13% to make up for the rate; sellers are cutting 5%. Who wins if rates fall: every buyer waiting on the sidelines. Who wins if prices fall: cash buyers, who don't care about rates at all. Most likely, neither breaks this winter. The freeze holds until the math works.

0:00 — The question

Part 1 showed the paradox: prices falling, nobody buying. Part 2 showed sellers starting to panic. Now the trilogy's closing question: what unfreezes a frozen housing market — sellers cutting deeper, or rates falling first?

0:06 — The rate path

In September 2026, the Federal Reserve raised rates again, to a target range of 3.75–4.00%. The dot-plot showed 16 of 18 officials expecting one more hike before the year ends. The Fed's own September projections have the funds rate at 4.1% at the end of 2026, 3.9% in 2027, and 3.6% in 2028 — a gradual glide, not a rescue.

0:16 — The twist

Here's the part that surprised me: even the optimists don't see real relief. The mortgage-rate forecasts put 2027 in the mid sixes — the Mortgage Bankers Association at 6.4–6.7%, Fannie Mae around 6.5%, Freddie Mac around 6.6%, the National Association of Realtors near 6.3%. Moody's Mark Zandi has said the market stays in a "deep freeze" until mortgage rates get "a 6% handle." The rate path, in short, is closed.

0:25 — The price math

So what about prices? Take a roughly $429,000 median home with 20% down: at 7.4%, principal and interest run about $2,376 a month; at 6%, about $2,058. To match the 6% payment at 7.4% rates, the price would have to fall about 13% — to roughly $373,000. Sellers, meanwhile, are cutting a median of 5%. That's less than half of what's needed.

0:36 — Who wins each way

If rates fall, every buyer waiting on the sidelines wins — demand returns, and sellers get their exit. If prices fall instead, cash buyers win — they don't care about rates at all. Two-thirds of homebuilders are already offering incentives and 38% are cutting prices (NAHB, September), the shallow end of the seller-side adjustment.

0:42 — The answer

Most likely, neither breaks this winter. Pending home sales are down 8.4% year over year (September), and Zillow's surveys show sidelined buyers waiting for early 2027 to re-enter. So the answer: the freeze holds until the math works. Sellers keep cutting, rates stay high, and the market waits.

What we're watching

The same two dials as Part 1: pending sales and price-cut depth. When pending sales turn, the freeze thaws — from the seller side, if cuts deepen past that 5% median, or from the rate side, if 2027 forecasts start sliding toward Zandi's 6% handle. Three videos, one answer: we'll keep watching the numbers.
