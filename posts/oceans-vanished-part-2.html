SCIENCE · 60 SECONDS · PART 2 OF 3

The Hidden World Underneath

October 2026

Your 60 seconds

Yesterday, the oceans vanished. Today, we climb down. Under the water hides the longest mountain range on Earth — the mid-ocean ridge, some 65,000 kilometers of volcanic peaks — and the Mariana Trench, a canyon 10,935 meters deep, enough to drop Mount Everest inside with two kilometers to spare. The floor is paved with salt and an estimated 3 million shipwrecks, and the old land bridges are walkable again. This is Part 2: the hidden world underneath.

0:00 — The hook

What if the oceans vanished? Yesterday, they did. Today, we climb down — and the first surprise is that the seafloor is not flat, empty, or boring. It is the most dramatic landscape on the planet, and almost nobody has ever seen it dry. A world of black volcanic mountains, bottomless canyons, and white plains has been sitting under a few kilometers of water the whole time.

0:20 — The context

Start with the mountains. Running through every ocean basin is the mid-ocean ridge, a volcanic mountain system about 65,000 kilometers long — roughly four times the Andes, the Rockies, and the Himalayas combined, and easily the longest mountain range on Earth. Standing dry, its jagged peaks and dead hydrothermal chimneys would look like a city built by giants and abandoned. Then there is the canyon. The Mariana Trench bottoms out at Challenger Deep, measured at about 10,935 meters. Mount Everest stands 8,849 meters tall — it would fit inside the trench with some two kilometers of air above its summit.

0:40 — The twist

The floor itself is stranger than the landmarks. The U.S. Geological Survey notes that if all the salt in the oceans were spread evenly over the land, it would form a layer more than 500 feet thick — a forty-story building of salt, and a hint of the white plains crusting the dry basins. Scattered across that floor, UNESCO estimates, are more than 3 million shipwrecks. The most famous of them, the Titanic, rests about 3,800 meters down in the North Atlantic. On the dry Earth, you would not dive to it. You would hike. And the shelves reopen the ancient roads: the Bering land bridge between Siberia and Alaska, and Doggerland between England and Europe — dry ground you could simply walk across.

1:00 — The takeaway

  • The mid-ocean ridge runs about 65,000 km — the longest mountain range on Earth, ~4× the Andes, Rockies, and Himalayas combined (WHOI).
  • Challenger Deep, in the Mariana Trench, reaches about 10,935 meters — Everest (8,849 m) fits inside with ~2 km to spare (UNH CCOM).
  • All the ocean's salt, spread over land, would bury the continents under more than 500 feet of it (USGS).
  • An estimated 3 million shipwrecks rest on the floor — including the Titanic, 3,800 m down (UNESCO; NOAA · WHOI).
