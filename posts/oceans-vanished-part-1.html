The Day the Oceans Vanished

October 2026

Your 60 seconds

Earth's oceans hold about 1.335 billion cubic kilometers of water — 96.5 percent of all the water on the planet. Remove them in a single night and the map redraws itself: beaches become cliff edges, every ship on Earth settles into the mud, and the exposed seabed more than triples the world's land. The average depth of the pit left behind is 3,688 meters. This is Part 1: day one of the dry Earth.

0:00 — The hook

What if every ocean on Earth vanished overnight? No warning, no water — just gone. It sounds like a disaster-movie premise, but the numbers underneath it are real. The oceans are not a feature of this planet; by volume, they practically are the planet's surface. The Pacific Ocean alone covers more area than all of Earth's landmasses combined.

0:20 — The context

According to the U.S. Geological Survey, the oceans contain roughly 1.335 billion cubic kilometers of water, which is about 96.5 percent of all the water on Earth — every river, lake, glacier, and aquifer makes up most of the rest. Take that away in one night and the first thing to go is the coastline itself. Beaches that took millennia to form become the rims of cliffs. Harbors dry out where they stand, and the world's fleet — fishing boats, container ships, aircraft carriers — simply settles into the silt.

0:40 — The twist

Here is the part nobody expects: the disaster comes with a land grab. The seafloor is not a small place — it is most of the planet. With the water gone, Earth's dry land more than triples, and continents grow skirts of rock and mud no human has ever stood on. But it is a pit, not a plain. NOAA puts the ocean's average depth at 3,688 meters — roughly four and a half Burj Khalifas stacked on top of each other. And then there is the quiet. No waves, no storms, no currents: day one of the dry Earth would be the quietest day in its history.

1:00 — The takeaway

  • The oceans hold 1.335 billion km³ of water — 96.5% of all water on Earth (USGS).
  • The Pacific Ocean alone is larger than all of Earth's land combined (NOAA).
  • The empty basin averages 3,688 meters deep — about 4.5 Burj Khalifas of empty air (NOAA).
  • With the water gone, dry land more than triples — and the real story is what the water was hiding underneath.

Sources

  • U.S. Geological Survey, Water Science School — "How much water is there on, in, and above the Earth?" (oceans ≈ 1.335 billion km³, 96.5% of all Earth's water)
  • NOAA Ocean Service — ocean facts: average ocean depth 3,688 meters (12,100 feet); the Pacific Ocean's surface area exceeds the total land area of all continents combined
