Why Nobody's Buying

October 2026 — United States

Your 60 seconds

Prices are falling. So why is nobody buying? Across 2026, the 30-year mortgage climbed from about 6.5% in the summer to 7.4% this week — the highest in almost three years. Sellers now outnumber buyers by 58%, supply hit a decade high, and one in five listings just took a price cut. But falling prices didn't bring buyers back — it froze them. The math still doesn't work: monthly payments run about $165 higher than a year ago on a typical home, and most owners won't sell because they'd have to trade a 3% mortgage for 7.4%. Cash buyers win; first-time buyers are at a record-low 21% of purchases.

0:00 — The paradox

For the first time in years, American housing favors buyers on paper: price cuts at a four-year high, sellers outnumbering buyers by 58% (Redfin, August), and 4.9 months of supply — the most in over a decade. Yet existing-home sales keep sliding and pending sales fell 4.1% in September, the steepest drop since March 2025.

0:10 — The rate arc

The 30-year fixed averaged about 6.5% in early summer, crossed 7% in late September for the first time since January 2025, then hit 7.28% the week ending October 1 and 7.40% by October 8 — seven consecutive weekly increases and the highest since November 2023 (Freddie Mac). Rates are roughly 70 basis points above last year's level.

0:25 — The lock-in effect

Most homeowners hold mortgages near 3% — and won't give them up. As Moody's Mark Zandi put it, demand is being hammered while supply stays weak because "homeowners remain locked in given their low existing mortgage rate." The market, he says, is in a "deep freeze" with "no getting out until mortgage rates have a 6% handle."

0:35 — The affordability math

On a roughly $429,000 median-priced home with 20% down, principal and interest run about $2,290 a month at 7.03% versus about $2,125 at 6.3% — roughly $165 more per month for the same house, before taxes and insurance. Falling asking prices haven't offset the financing cost.

0:45 — Who wins, who's out

Cash buyers and anyone with equity win the flip. First-time buyers are at a record-low 21% of purchases at a record median age of 40 (NAR). Mortgage purchase applications are down 14% year over year (MBA, September 30).

What we're watching

Pending sales — down 4% in September. When that turns, the freeze thaws. And in Dallas, where half of all listings just cut their price (Parcl HQ, October 1), what happens when sellers start to panic.
