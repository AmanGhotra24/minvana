SCIENCE · 60 SECONDS · PART 3 OF 3

Then the Rain Stops

October 2026

Your 60 seconds

The oceans are gone and the seabed is dry — but the real killer was never the empty ocean floor. It is the sky. About 90 percent of the moisture in the air comes from evaporating water, most of it from the oceans, so the rain stops. Ocean plankton produce about half the oxygen we breathe, and over 3 billion people depend on the sea for protein. This is Part 3, the finale: how the dry Earth dies — and what survives it.

0:00 — The hook

The oceans are gone. The seabed is dry. But the real killer isn't the empty seabed — it's the sky. Here's how the Earth dies, and what survives. The vanishing was an event; what follows is a process, and it starts above your head, in the first weeks, when the clouds simply stop coming back.

0:20 — The context

The rain stops first. The U.S. Geological Survey puts it plainly: most of the moisture in the atmosphere — about 90 percent — comes from water evaporating off oceans, seas, lakes, and rivers, and the oceans supply the overwhelming share. No ocean, no water cycle worth the name. Rivers shrink to mud, lakes evaporate into salt pans, and the weather machine that has watered every farm in history winds down. Then the air itself changes. NOAA credits tiny ocean phytoplankton with producing about half of Earth's oxygen. That factory is now closed forever. The oxygen already in the sky would not vanish overnight — but nothing is making half of it anymore.

0:40 — The twist

And the hunger comes fast. The United Nations counts over 3 billion people who depend on the ocean as a primary source of protein, and scientists estimate some 2.2 million species live in it — gone, most of them, in weeks. Without the ocean carrying heat around the planet, the land turns brutal: days that scorch, nights that freeze. But here is the part that surprises everyone: something survives. Deep underground, in groundwater and fractured rock, live microbes that never needed sunlight, or oceans, at all. Life retreats into the dark — where, billions of years ago, it began.

1:00 — The takeaway

  • About 90% of the moisture in the air comes from evaporating water — overwhelmingly the oceans (USGS).
  • Ocean phytoplankton produce about half the oxygen we breathe — and that production ends (NOAA).
  • Over 3 billion people depend on the ocean for protein; an estimated ~2 million marine species lose their world (UN; Mora et al., 2011).
  • We have mapped the Moon better than our own seafloor — only about 26% of it is charted (Seabed 2030).

Sources
