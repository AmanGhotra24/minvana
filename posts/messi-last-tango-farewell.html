Messi's Last Tango: The Farewell After 21 Years

85,000 fans, one stadium, one goodbye. Sent off on his debut in 2005, a world champion in 2022 — Lionel Messi closes the greatest Argentina career ever played.

60 SEC Your 60 Seconds

  • On October 6, 2026, Lionel Messi plays his 208th and final game for Argentina — a farewell friendly against Benin at the Estadio Más Monumental in Buenos Aires, dubbed "The Last Tango."
  • Some 85,000 fans are expected; tickets sold out in under two hours. A special commemorative jersey was created, and members of the 2022 World Cup-winning squad were invited.
  • The numbers: 207 caps and 125 goals — both all-time Argentina records — plus 68 assists. Eight Ballon d'Ors, the most in history.
  • The trophies: Copa América 2021, Finalissima 2022, the 2022 FIFA World Cup, Copa América 2024. Six World Cups, 34 matches (an all-time record), 21 goals.
  • The arc: sent off minutes into his senior debut vs Hungary in August 2005; lost finals in 2007, 2014, 2015 and 2016; briefly retired from internationals in 2016, then reversed it — and led Argentina through the most successful period in its modern history.

0:00 The Last Tango

There is a particular cruelty in farewells that are scheduled. For 21 years, Lionel Messi simply showed up for Argentina — through the teenage prodigy years, through the finals lost, through the retirement that wasn't. On October 6, 2026, he shows up one last time: a friendly against Benin at the Monumental, arranged for no competitive reason except that 85,000 people needed a place to say goodbye together.

Tickets sold out in under two hours. The Argentine federation created a special jersey for the occasion. Rodrigo De Paul and Giovani Lo Celso — teammates from the golden years — are in the squad, and the champions of Qatar 2022 were invited back to stand with him.

0:15 The numbers

Messi leaves as Argentina's most-capped player (207 appearances) and all-time top scorer (125 goals), with 68 assists — all national records. He played at six World Cups, appearing in 34 matches and scoring 21 goals, both all-time records. He won the World Cup's Golden Ball twice, in 2014 and 2022. His eight Ballon d'Ors are the most any footballer has ever won.

But the numbers were never the point of the Messi-Argentina story. For most of his career, they were the indictment: all that genius, and nothing to show for it in sky blue and white.

0:30 Heartbreak, then redemption

His senior debut, on August 17, 2005 against Hungary, lasted barely a minute before he was sent off. What followed was a decade of near-misses that genuinely wounded him: the 2014 World Cup final lost to Germany in extra time, Copa América final defeats on penalties to Chile in 2015 and 2016. After the 2016 loss, he announced his international retirement — and meant it, until an outpouring of public emotion convinced him to return.

The return made history. In 2021, Argentina beat Brazil 1-0 at the Maracanã to win the Copa América — ending a 28-year trophy drought and giving Messi his first major senior trophy with his country. In 2022 came the Finalissima, and then Qatar: seven goals, three assists, and a final against France so dramatic it is already spoken of as the greatest ever played. At 35, he had completed football.

0:50 The goodbye

The 2026 World Cup final slipped away — Spain won 1-0 after extra time — and in late August Messi announced what everyone sensed: "I gave it my all; I have nothing left to give, and besides, there are great young players coming up who deserve their chance."

He will continue playing club football with Inter Miami. But the number 10 shirt of Argentina — the shirt of a 21-year identity — gets folded one final time on Tuesday night in Buenos Aires. Gracias, Leo.
