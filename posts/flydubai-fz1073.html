Stabbed Mid-Flight, 14,000-Foot Plunge: What Happened on flydubai FZ1073

A Dubai–Tel Aviv flight diverted to Saudi Arabia after a violent cockpit altercation sent the jet into a steep dive. Here is what is confirmed — and what investigators still don't know.

60 SEC Your 60 Seconds

  • flydubai flight FZ1073, a Boeing 737 MAX 8 flying Dubai to Tel Aviv, diverted to Tabuk, Saudi Arabia on September 30 after a violent altercation broke out between the captain and the co-pilot mid-flight.
  • The jet plunged roughly 14,000–17,000 feet in about a minute and transmitted the 7500 squawk code for unlawful interference. The stabbed captain — Indian pilot Smit Machchhar — managed to open the cockpit door; passengers and crew then overpowered the co-pilot. Two off-duty flydubai pilots on board helped land the aircraft. Everyone on board survived.
  • Both pilots were hospitalized and the co-pilot was detained by Saudi authorities. The cause is under official investigation: flydubai says it remains unknown and has warned against speculation, while Israeli officials say all scenarios are being examined.

0:00 The Hook

Two hours into a routine morning flight from Dubai to Tel Aviv, something went wrong inside the cockpit. Flight FZ1073, carrying more than 170 people — most of them Israeli nationals, according to Reuters — suddenly fell out of its cruise. Flight-tracking data showed the Boeing 737 MAX 8 shedding roughly 14,000 feet in under 30 seconds, then transmitting a general emergency signal followed by squawk 7500, the code pilots use to indicate unlawful interference.

The aircraft diverted to Tabuk in northwestern Saudi Arabia, landing safely at around 8:45 a.m. local time. What happened in the cockpit during those minutes is now the subject of investigations in three countries.

0:20 The Context

Here is what officials and the airline have confirmed. During the flight, the two pilots became involved in a physical altercation, and the co-pilot stabbed the captain, according to statements from the Israeli prime minister's office citing passenger accounts. The captain — Smit Machchhar, an Indian pilot from Mumbai who previously flew with SpiceJet for over 11 years — was seriously injured but managed to trigger the cockpit door release, allowing crew and passengers to enter and restrain the co-pilot.

Two additional flydubai pilots who happened to be traveling on the aircraft assisted, and the plane was landed safely in Tabuk. Both operating pilots were taken to hospital; Machchhar underwent surgery and is reported to be in stable condition, according to the Indian embassy in Saudi Arabia. The co-pilot was arrested and is being questioned by Saudi authorities, Israeli prime minister Benjamin Netanyahu said.

0:40 The Twist

This is where confirmed fact ends and competing characterizations begin. Three Israeli officials, speaking anonymously, told Reuters the incident is being assessed as a possible attempted terror attack, with one identifying the co-pilot as an Omani national. Netanyahu himself stopped short of that label. Separately, French outlet Le Monde reported that investigators are not ruling out other scenarios either.

flydubai, for its part, has said the underlying reasons "remain unknown," warned against "premature speculation," and suspended flights to and from Israel while the investigation continues. In response, Israel said it will tighten security vetting of pilots on all flights into the country, including those operated by foreign carriers.

1:00 The Takeaway

Aviation incidents this serious are investigated by the book — flight data, cockpit voice recordings, and witness accounts across the UAE, Saudi Arabia, and Israel. Until those findings land, the confirmed story is this: a mid-air crisis, a wounded pilot who got the door open, passengers and crew who intervened, and more than 170 people who walked off a plane in Tabuk.
